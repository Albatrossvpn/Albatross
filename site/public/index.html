Why Albatross

Built to connect, not to be configured.

Everything that usually takes a settings screen happens on its own.

Instant failover

A connection is a group of servers in one location behind a selector. The one in use is watched continuously; backups are checked in the background. A drop moves traffic to the best backup without restarting anything, and your location does not change.

The fastest location, measured

Servers are ranked by real HTTPS requests through them, not by a ping. Choose the fastest location overall, or any location with servers available, each shown with its server count and best latency.

Secure by default

Plain HTTP, FTP, Telnet and unprotected mail ports are refused, because whoever carries them can read them. Name lookups go through the tunnel. On Android, one tap opens the system's always-on VPN switches.

Nine protocols, chosen for you

VLESS, VMess, Trojan, Shadowsocks, Hysteria 2, TUIC, AnyTLS, WireGuard and OpenVPN, with REALITY, ShadowTLS, XHTTP and more underneath. Leave it on Automatic, or fix one.

Ad and tracker blocking

A list built every day from the major public filters plus regional ad networks, about half a million names, blocked at the name level with an allowlist that keeps sign-ins, payments and app stores working.

Local sites, direct

Sites of your region open directly instead of through the VPN, so banking, shopping and government services stay fast. Names the VPN carries are learned and added as they are found.

How it works

One button. A lot of work behind it.

It learns your network

Albatross measures how your network filters right now, from ordinary blocklists to the hardest allowlists, and ranks every way out accordingly. What works for others on your operator helps you start faster, using counts only, never addresses.

It tests before it trusts

Every server gets a real HTTPS request through it, with its exit checked, before it is used. Servers that forge certificates are dropped for good.

It keeps backups warm

While you browse, backups in the same location are checked in the background. When the server in use fails, the switch is a change of selection, not a reconnect.

For the hardest networks

Ways out that work when nothing else does.

When a network cuts every ordinary path, Albatross reaches for the ones that survive, in the order they are most likely to work.

Through DNSA tunnel inside ordinary DNS queries, through the network's own resolver, for the days when only names still resolve.
Through pingA carrier inside ICMP echo packets, for when both TCP and UDP to foreign addresses are cut.
Port 53 and port 80Servers that answer on the ports allowlists leave open, over UDP and TCP.
Real TLS handshakesREALITY and ShadowTLS make a genuine handshake with a well-known site, so the connection looks like what it imitates.
Big networks as the frontCloudflare edge addresses that your network still lets through are found and used, and WARP over MASQUE, over QUIC or over TCP when UDP is throttled.
Tor bridges as the last resortSnowflake, WebTunnel, obfs4, meek and Conjure, fetched fresh, when every server is blocked.

Privacy, honestly

What we guarantee, and what we cannot.

A VPN that promises everything is hiding something. Here is exactly how Albatross treats your data.

Guaranteed

  • Traffic is encrypted from your device to the server; HTTPS sites stay encrypted to the site.
  • Unencrypted traffic is refused, so nothing the app carries can be read in plain text on the way.
  • Name lookups for the sites you visit go through the tunnel.
  • The account service receives a username, a password at sign-in, and counts of bytes and seconds. It never receives the sites you visit.
  • Shared statistics are counts only: no addresses of servers or devices, no install number.

Not guaranteed

  • A server operator can still see which sites you connect to and how much data you move. Albatross does not claim anonymity or "no logs", because no VPN can make that promise on your behalf.
  • Account limits are enforced by the app, so they stop honest overuse, not someone who rebuilds the client.
  • The serverless fallback, used only when no server works, opens name-blocked sites through your own line and hides nothing from your network's operator. The app says so when it is in use.

Questions

Frequently asked.

Is Albatross free?

The app is free to download and open source. Accounts, where a build asks for one, are provided by the operator of your Albatross service, who sets the allowances.

Why is there no server list?

Because choosing is the app's job. It shows how many servers are available, where, and how fast, and keeps a group of them ready behind every connection. You pick a location; it picks and re-picks the server.

Which protocol should I choose?

Automatic. Albatross measures your network and ranks protocols by what works on it right now. Fix a protocol only if you know you need one.

Does it work on heavily filtered networks?

That is what it is built for. Beyond ordinary servers it carries traffic through DNS, through ping, through Cloudflare's network, through Tor bridges, and as a last resort through your own line with the TLS handshake fragmented. It tries them in the order they are most likely to work on your network.

Can I run my own servers?

Yes. One script sets up a server that offers every protocol the app runs, on a free cloud tier, and the app can be built to use it. See the repository's deploy folder.

How do updates work?

The app checks a signed manifest at start and every six hours, downloads the new version through its own connection, verifies its SHA-256 and installs it in place. A required update stops older versions from connecting until they update.
