Why Albatross

Built to connect, not to be configured.

Everything that usually takes a settings screen happens on its own.

Instant failover

Every connection keeps several servers ready in the same location. If the one in use drops, your traffic moves to the best backup without reconnecting, and your location does not change.

The fastest location, measured

Servers are ranked by real HTTPS requests through them, not by a ping. Choose the fastest location overall, or any location with servers available, each shown with its server count and best latency.

Secure by default

Connections that would travel unencrypted are refused, so no one along the way can read them. Name lookups go through the tunnel too. On Android, one tap opens the system's always-on VPN switches.

Nine protocols, chosen for you

Nine modern protocols, including VLESS with REALITY, Hysteria 2, WireGuard and OpenVPN. Albatross uses the one your network lets through. Leave it on Automatic, or choose one yourself.

Ad and tracker blocking

A list built every day from the major public filters plus regional ad networks, about half a million names, blocked at the name level with an allowlist that keeps sign-ins, payments and app stores working.

Local sites, direct

Sites of your region open directly instead of through the VPN, so banking, shopping and government services stay fast. Names the VPN carries are learned and added as they are found.

Stay close

The people who matter, one tap away.

Video calls, messages and voice notes keep working on networks that try to stop them. Albatross picks the way out that your network still allows, and changes it the moment that stops working.

  • Calls that do not drop. A failing server is replaced within a second, without reconnecting.
  • Nothing to set up. Install, sign in, press the button.
Anywhere

Connected on mobile data, café Wi-Fi and everything between.

When you move from one network to another, Albatross measures the new one and adjusts on its own, so the connection follows you instead of breaking.

  • Local sites stay fast. Banking, shopping and government services open directly.
  • Lighter on the battery. Scanning slows down on phones once a good server is found.
How it works

One button. A lot of work behind it.

It learns your network

Albatross measures how your network filters right now, from ordinary blocklists to the hardest allowlists, and ranks every way out accordingly. If you turn on sharing, what works for others on your operator helps you start faster, using counts only, never addresses.

It tests before it trusts

Every server gets a real HTTPS request through it, with its exit checked, before it is used. Any server caught tampering with secure connections is dropped for good.

It keeps backups warm

While you browse, backups in the same location are checked in the background. When the server in use fails, the switch is a change of selection, not a reconnect.

For the hardest networks

Ways out that work when nothing else does.

When a network cuts every ordinary path, Albatross reaches for the ones that survive, in the order they are most likely to work.

Through DNSA tunnel inside ordinary DNS queries, through the network's own resolver, for the days when only names still resolve.
Through pingHidden inside ping packets, for networks that block almost everything else.
Port 53 and port 80Servers that answer on the ports allowlists leave open, over UDP and TCP.
Real TLS handshakesREALITY and ShadowTLS make a genuine handshake with a well-known site, so the connection looks like what it imitates.
Big networks as the frontThrough Cloudflare's network, using the addresses your network still allows, and through Cloudflare WARP.
Tor bridges as the last resortTor's bridge network, fetched fresh, when every server is blocked.

Privacy

How Albatross treats your data.

What is protected on the way, and the little the account service ever sees.

GuaranteedWhat Albatross does for you

  • Signing up needs only a username and password: no email, no phone number, no real name.
  • Traffic is encrypted from your device to the server; HTTPS sites stay encrypted to the site.
  • Unencrypted traffic is refused, so nothing the app carries can be read in plain text on the way.
  • Name lookups for the sites you visit go through the tunnel.
  • The account service receives a username, a password at sign-in, and counts of bytes and seconds. It never receives the sites you visit.
  • Sharing what works on your network is off unless you turn on “Learn from other users”, and it sends counts only: no server or device addresses, no username, no install number.
  • If you buy data in the Telegram bot, Telegram takes the payment in Stars and we never see your payment details. We keep the payment's record (your Telegram number, the package, the account) for two years, for refunds, and the account remembers its buyer's number so that only that Telegram account can ask for a new password.
  • The support chat and the Telegram bot are answered by an AI assistant. When a chat ends, the conversation and a summary go to our team by email; nothing else of it is kept, except that the bot holds a chat until about half an hour after your last message, and a ticket until it is closed. Questions

Frequently asked.

Is Albatross free?

Yes. The app is free to download. To connect, you sign in with an Albatross account: just a username and password, with no email, name or phone number. An account can have a data or time allowance, which the app and your account page show.

Why is there no server list?

Because choosing is the app's job. It shows how many servers are available, where, and how fast, and keeps a group of them ready behind every connection. You pick a location; it picks and re-picks the server.

Which protocol should I choose?

Automatic. Albatross measures your network and ranks protocols by what works on it right now. Fix a protocol only if you know you need one. The app carries VLESS, VMess, Trojan, Shadowsocks, Hysteria 2, TUIC, AnyTLS, WireGuard and OpenVPN, with REALITY, ShadowTLS and XHTTP underneath.

Does it work on heavily filtered networks?

That is what it is built for. Beyond ordinary servers it carries traffic through DNS, through ping, through Cloudflare's network, through Tor bridges, and as a last resort directly over your own connection, split so that filters cannot tell which site it is for. It tries them in the order they are most likely to work on your network.

Can I run my own servers?

Yes. One script sets up a server that offers every protocol the app runs, on a free cloud tier, and the app can be built to use it.

Does it work on iPhone and iPad?

Yes, since version 1.0.0. There is no Albatross app in the App Store; instead your account page hands your servers to a connection app you install from the App Store (V2Box and Streisand are free), with one tap for all servers, one country, or the edge. They show up under the Albatross name and refresh by themselves. Add the page to your Home Screen and it opens like an app.

How do updates work?

The app checks a signed manifest at start and every six hours, downloads the new version through its own connection, verifies its SHA-256 and installs it in place. A required update stops older versions from connecting until they update.

Ready when you need it.

Install it once. On the day your network closes the usual doors, Albatross already knows the other ones.
